## No-mate theorem and direct binding

If A is checking a King at k and all A offsets have one parity, every attacked
square has k's colour. Pick on-board perpendicular orthogonal neighbours u,v.
Neither is attacked by A. A legal opposing King cannot guard both without
being adjacent to k. If A occupies u and v is guarded, u cannot also be guarded:
the checked King captures A, and attacks are recomputed after its removal.
Otherwise an unguarded empty neighbour is an escape. Bare Kings cannot legally
check each other. This includes both colour-preserving and colour-flipping A;
it does not assume that A's own colour is invariant.

The rule parser rejects extra semantics, nonroyal/altered Kings, non-Leaps,
promotion, hands/drops, non-draw stalemate/repetition, auxiliary effects and
nonstandard board scope. State admission requires two nonadjacent Kings and
at most one ordinary actor. Captures stay in that domain; other terminals draw
and a finite clock guarantees termination. Thus admitted full histories have
DRAW and all legal actions DRAW. Mixed parity is not a positive mate prediction.

Seven supplied masks each test6720 labelled three-actor placements, rejecting
2352 adjacent-King placements. Across masks:30576 admitted placements,
6072 checked placements,20104 actual public-Core reply edges and2304 capture-A
reply edges. Primitive/semantic attack and legal reply sets agree. Each mask
also checks312 bare-King placements/1152 actual edges. These are local complete
placement checks; repeated geometry across masks is not independent data.

Independently of parity, a bare King cannot checkmate the other royal King.
With only one ordinary actor, only its owner can ever win. This gives[0,1]
to that owner's move and[-1,0]to the bare side's move, until literal terminals
or stronger certificates settle the result. This is a continuation bound, not
a learned value or evidence of useful material scaling.
