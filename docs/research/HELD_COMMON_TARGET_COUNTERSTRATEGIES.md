## Held Pawn: task value0

With n=1, a first King move leaves source held. Its final possible drop cannot
capture. A first Pawn drop can capture the unchanged e6 target next turn only
from e5. If dropped e5, enemy e6-e5 legally captures it immediately. No own
board-source remains to capture on the last own turn.

For every other legal Pawn drop, choose enemy King i8-i9. This is safe: the
only dropped native Pawn that attacks i9 would stand i8, already occupied;
own King is far away. A Pawn checking i8 from i7 does not attack i9. Target
stays e6 and the source's next action cannot capture it. Promotion occurs
after the native movement/capture geometry, so a final promotion cannot supply
a lateral capture in that same move. These cases cover every root action.

For n=2 exchangeable source identities, the e5 drop leaves board mass1/2,
which is captured, and held mass1/2, which cannot capture on its final drop.
Other drops have the same public counterstrategy. Hence task value0 also.

## Held Knight: task value0

A native Knight can capture e6 next turn only after a first drop at d4 or f4.
Enemy e6-e5 is legal in either case and shifts the target out of that Knight's
capture set. Neither of these dropped Knights attacks enemy King i8, so this
Pawn reply leaves the enemy King safe.

For every other Knight drop, keep target e6 fixed by moving the enemy King.
At i8 it has five on-board neighboring destinations. The dropped Knight can
attack at most two of them and occupy at most one; the distant own King attacks
none. Thus at least two destinations are empty and safe. Choose one such
legal King move. Target remains e6, which the source cannot capture on its
remaining native move. Promotion is applied afterward, not before capture.
First own King moves again leave source held, incapable of a last-action drop
capture. The proof covers n=1 and n=2 without a new physical experiment.
