## Method lesson and next decision

[TDLeaf's original equations3–4and9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/9901001) combine prediction differences, propagated credit and leaf derivatives; they do not identify own-gradient counts with learning progress. Its continuous-evaluator minimax discussion does not make integer/selective search differentiable. [Settles, Craven and Friedland's actual-cost experiments](https://burrsettles.com/pub/settles.nips08ws.pdf), section3/Figure8, show that query-count improvements can vanish against paid time and that a greedy utility/cost heuristic can itself lose. Their human-label setting is an analogy, not evidence of GenericChess performance.

Keep optional distribution-aware sampling and cheap static/learned/Unit controls. Measure actual downstream learning/utility before selecting a new sampler or budget, under existing AUX ownership and nonduplicated claims. The measured Core optimization serves current complete-state consumers; it does not solve generic prices, validate every generated rule, increase Elo or change the long-term task definition.

A subsequent actual consumer inventory finds that Session.submit also owns before/
after identity keys, ActionRecord and exact search witnesses, committing only
after all Core calls succeed. Existing lazy handles are bound to the originating
GameState identity and belong to search; Session has no handle-submit contract.
Thus another enumeration saving is not a drop-in consumer switch. Defer that new
API unless a real downstream workload pays its validation/maintenance cost; do
not privately assign Session fields or equate a handle child with a full commit.
This inventory, rather than another timing repetition, starts the next decision.
