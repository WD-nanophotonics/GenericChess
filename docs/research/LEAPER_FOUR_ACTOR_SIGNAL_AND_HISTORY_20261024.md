## Scoped incoming-barrier reduction

Assumptions are admission requirements, not a generic GameSession quotient:

* Complete finite alternating position-local legal transition graph; no hidden
  history-dependent legality, declarations, promotion, drops, aux changes,
  continuous-check loss, no-progress or other adjudication.
* Repetition identities are injective on the complete represented Positions.
* The sole history stops are automatic repetition-to-DRAW at q visits and a
  finite ply-to-DRAW clock. Literal mate/stalemate authority and precedence are
  preserved. The current source is a verified ongoing full history.

For a current root v with incoming counts c, replace every nonterminal
u!=v having c(u)=q-1 by a DRAW sink. Keep literal terminals unchanged and v
playable: its current arrival has already happened. Solve ordinary reachability
WDL and strict minimum-win/maximum-loss ranks on that modified graph. At
remaining H, return its nonzero root value exactly when rank<=H; otherwise DRAW.

Why this is exact for the admitted WDL problem:

1. A winning rank strategy chooses a strictly lower-rank child at the winner's
   turns; every defender choice also has lower rank. Consequently every path
   terminates within the rank, never revisits a Position, never returns to v,
   and cannot accumulate an additional repetition stop at an incoming count
   <=q-2. The other q-1 barriers are already DRAW sinks, so a winning strategy
   avoids them. This lifts a rank<=H win to the actual complete history.
2. Each player's positional draw-safety strategy in the modified graph still
   avoids losing terminals when additional actual repetition/clock stops end
   play earlier as DRAW. Thus a modified-graph DRAW stays DRAW.
3. The finite-clock Bellman game on that graph is DRAW when a decisive rank
   exceeds H. Adding further draw-only stops cannot create either player's
   forced win from that DRAW game. The current-root future-return stop, which
   was omitted from the graph, is one such additional stop. This proves the
   cutoff case without pretending it is UNKNOWN or a fixed-opponent return.

The method recomputes ranks after blocking; applying the old physical ranks
unchanged would be unsound. It does not model policy-dependent probabilities,
warm TT, timed search decisions or arbitrary generated rules.
