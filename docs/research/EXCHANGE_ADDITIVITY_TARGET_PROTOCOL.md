# Frozen additivity-target audit

The candidate gives a single focal token's probability of securing an exchange.
Before interpreting such probabilities as additive material prices, separate
three targets: success of at least one available actor, the number of individually
successful actors, and actual net token custody. These need not be the same.
This is not a new Shapley allocation, physical removal map, score population or
coefficient fit, and does not demand a uniquely rule-chosen measure.

Use ONLY the already frozen Chess nonterminal support frame. It has own Rooks
at(3,3) and(0,0), an enemy Pawn at(0,3), and the unchanged full-inventory board.
Permit first actions from either declared R source; all pieces remain present,
and all actions/replies have the same original custody baseline/task semantics.
Enumerate once and partition actions by source. Restricting control is an
optional-action eligibility intervention, NOT deleting a physical piece.
Reproduce the original focal(3,3) action/reply evidence exactly.

Prediction before execution: either R can independently capture the SAME Pawn
at(0,3) and retain positive custody after every reply, with ongoing states.
Both individual successes are1, their joint turn success is1, the count of
individually successful actors is2, while each capture/reply yields custody+1.
Replay both selected captures and every reply, preserving the victim identity
as owner/type/square; no new positions, repairs, samples or source weights.

Also audit two explicitly equiprobable abstract environments, not game-state
samples. Overlap law: (A,B) successes are(1,1),(0,0). Disjoint law: (1,0),(0,1).
Both laws have identical marginal probabilities1/2 each, and expected count1;
union probabilities differ1/2 versus1. Under eligibility restriction, marginal
union contributions are0 versus1/2. Verify by exact fractions. These are chosen
finite measures illustrating target distinctions, not strategic population claims.

For fixed physical contexts and unchanged actions/payoffs, joint eligibility
success is OR/max over individual binary successes. Its mean is generally not
their sum. The expected number of individually successful actors IS their sum
by linearity, with no independence assumption. Neither equation on its own
makes this count strategic utility, a custody count, a physical deletion value,
or a type-local price transferable across inventories/populations.

Limit one real board, two actor groups, 2,000 materializations, ten seconds,
external15-second timeout; public replays count too. Keep every reply and fail
on incomplete coverage. No other R support examples, engine profile, Xiangqi
human values, framework changes or extra workers.
