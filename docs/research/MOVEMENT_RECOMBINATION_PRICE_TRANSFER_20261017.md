## What was useful in CHAT_Comments and the primary literature

The adopted lesson is to separate representation compatibility, decision changes and actual utility, and to isolate transfer axes before a larger architecture. This produced the movement-only experiment, identity controls, exact mapping recipe and linked-family uncertainty accounting. The comments are suggestions, not new policy or an instruction to build embeddings/TC/provers.

[Towards a General Transfer Approach for Policy-Value Networks](https://www.ericpiette.com/papers/tmlr23.pdf), §3/AppendixB, separates equivalent encoded channels from equal strategic implications and uses explicit mappings. Its transfer evaluations distinguish zero-shot from fine-tuning and scratch, including whether source pretraining is charged. Our inference is to make the simple type-ID recipe explicit and avoid interpreting its success or failure as that of every rule-conditioned representation; we implement no network or ludemes mapper here.

[Polygames](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09832), §2.1, uses fully convolutional heads/global pooling to accept multiple board sizes; its cited Hex example still discusses fine-tuning. Shape compatibility is therefore not a guarantee of zero-shot rule transfer. [Graph-Based Domain Mapping](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~pstone/Papers/bib2html-links/ECML07-rulegraphs.pdf), §3–4, distinguishes matching renamed descriptions from relating changed tasks and their values. Our bounded rename control and non-equivalent movement variants reflect that distinction; no graph framework is introduced.
