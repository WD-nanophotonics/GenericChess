### Own finite-terminal-payoff qualification

Do not mechanically apply a horizon-squared cumulative-cost slogan to terminal-only WDL. For ONE fixed root law, complete rule-terminated games, the same fixed opponent, deterministic teacher policy and a frozen student policy, pad terminals with a shared absorbing no-op to at mostT own decisions. Couple the two plays to use identical opponent randomness while their histories agree. Let epsilon_t be the student's probability of disagreeing with the teacher's action at a time-t state under the TEACHER occupancy law. The first divergence probability is at most sum_t epsilon_t: every first-divergence prefix has no greater mass than its corresponding teacher prefix. If there is no divergence the terminal utility is identical. Since player-0 WDL lies in[-1,1],

abs(E[Z_student]-E[Z_teacher]) <= 2*min(1,sum_t epsilon_t).

The same first-divergence union argument can use STUDENT occupancy instead; neither occupancy is furnished by the arbitrary diagnostic corpus. With a stochastic teacher, the conditional total-variation discrepancy under an appropriate action coupling replaces deterministic disagreement; independently sampled action mismatch is not itself that TV distance. This is an own elementary coupling result, not a newly verified paper theorem or an empirical quality estimate. It gives closeness to the specified teacher, never superiority to the teacher, minimax truth or cross-rule transfer. It assumes complete actual scoring outcomes, matched roots/opponent and the relevant full history/policy memory; timed incomplete queries and resource-censored games do not acquire WDL targets through this argument. No epsilon or resulting bound is numerically certified for existing runs.

The paper's cumulative task-cost bound addresses a different target and can remain horizon-squared. Terminal WDL has fixed range, so the damage after a first divergence is bounded by2 for this particular task. This distinction prevents both an exaggerated intrinsic impossibility claim and an unsupported claim that arbitrary high corpus agreement proves learning/play gains.

## Rule-conditioned reuse is not reward-weight transfer

[Barreto et al.2017](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/file/350db081a661525235354dd3e19b8c05-Paper.pdf), sections2-4/theorems1-2, factors a policy value into successor features and a linear immediate-reward weight vector. Its transfer family fixes the dynamics/action/state spaces and varies reward weights; the discounted GPI guarantee uses uniformly qualified policy-value approximation errors. Changing dynamics may require relearning the dynamics module, as the paper itself notes. Read definitions, decomposition and transfer/theorem discussion, not supplementary proofs or experimental replication. This source does not rule out other transfer methods or later work on changed dynamics.

[Schaul et al.2015](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/schaul15.pdf), sections2-3 and5, conditions a value approximator on goals, which define pseudo-rewards/discounts in the described MDP. It studies supervised and reward-driven goal generalization, with explicit exploration/approximation limitations. Read architecture/setup and reinforcement-learning procedures; no empirical reproduction or appendix read. A goal input is a possible conditional representation, not proof that arbitrary changed executable rules preserve the original transition process or learned values. Neither method is adopted here; private source hashes are recorded in transfer-source-scope.json.

Own consumer comparison:

* The actual material parameters being learned are coefficients of a SEARCH approximation. The true game payoff remains actual W/L/D. Changing a leaf price changes the search policy, not the task's immediate reward vector in the cited successor-feature family.
* Their transition-reward features are not automatically our selected PV's material count differences. Encoding win/draw/loss reward indicators would still require the rule/policy-dependent future-outcome predictions; the factorization does not supply those from counts alone.
* Changing Leap rules, effects, terminal conditions or history legality generally changes transitions and/or action domains. Same type names/counts do not establish a fixed environment. Checkpoint fingerprint rejection is an appropriate current safety boundary; a future explicit semantic transfer would need its own justified mapping rather than disabling the guard.
* The GPI bounds cited are discounted and concern specified policy Q values, not heuristic PV scores or arbitrary WDL intervals. Gamma1 finite complete games need their own analysis; no parameter is changed merely to fit a discounted theorem. An exploratory learning test need not first certify a uniform oracle error, but cannot claim that particular theorem without its premises.
* A rule-conditioned function over a disjoint union of rule-specific states is mathematically possible. That observation alone gives no unseen-rule generalization guarantee, task-similarity metric, intrinsic piece prices or measured lower cost. Reusing one learner implementation across rules and reusing one learned checkpoint across changed rules are distinct claims.

Disposition: these original transfer results do NOT justify an automatic cross-RuleSet transplant of the existing material checkpoint. They suggest separable questions about reward/context representation and dynamics-dependent knowledge; no new model, conditional encoder, SF library, training run or transfer experiment is started. The current explicit RuleSet binding remains.
