## Conditional finite-history mathematical specification (own derivation)

This section specifies a hypothetical policy-evaluation baseline only. It changes no learner, policy, budget or production setting, and is not a new convergence claim for the actual consumer.

Let S be all reachable complete-history own-decision states of ONE fixed finite-horizon RuleSet, with fixed root law nu and fixed policies for both sides. Integrate the moves between own observations into a substochastic transition matrix K on S. Terminal payoff enters r; terminal continuation value is zero. Require complete rule-terminated sequences without resource censoring and a stationary kernel independent of updated parameters. Full histories strictly increase in length, so K is nilpotent for a bounded-horizon rule. Material features may be any fixed map of these states; they need not themselves be Markov.

Expected visit counts satisfy d^T=nu^T+d^T K; discard unvisited states so d_i>0. Write D=diag(d), kappa=1-K*1. For any vector y,

2*y^T*D*(I-K)*y = sum_ij d_i*K_ij*(y_i-y_j)^2 + sum_i nu_i*y_i^2 + sum_i d_i*kappa_i*y_i^2.

Expansion proves the identity using the visit balance. Every term is nonnegative. A zero sum forces y=0 at starts and along every positive-probability path, hence on all reachable states. This supplies strict positivity without assuming that a feature vector uniquely identifies history. It also avoids an unjustified shortcut from one strict row to strict diagonal dominance of an arbitrary reducible matrix.

For an affine fixed-unit LINEAR value v=a+Phi*theta, let A=Phi^T*D*(I-K)*Phi and b=Phi^T*D*(r+K*a-a). If Phi has full column rank on visited states, the symmetric part of A is positive definite. The expected frozen complete-episode TD(0) increment is b-A*theta. This is an expected drift identity; the deterministic mean iteration is stable for sufficiently small step. A sufficient Euclidean condition is 0<eta<2*mu/L^2 with mu=lambda_min((A+A^T)/2)>0 and L=||A||_2. It does not certify eta=.05, stochastic almost-sure convergence, raw-material-to-WDL units, or empirical strength. Normalizing counts per episode versus per observation rescales A and therefore the step condition.

For 0<=lambda<=1 define K_lambda=(1-lambda)*K*(I-lambda*K)^(-1). Nilpotence makes every inverse a finite sum, including lambda1, when K_lambda=0. Its associated start mass is nu_lambda^T=nu^T*(I-lambda*K)^(-1), and d^T*(I-K_lambda)=nu_lambda^T. Thus the same energy identity applies to K_lambda. The expected complete-episode drift has

A_lambda=Phi^T*D*(I-lambda*K)^(-1)*(I-K)*Phi,

b_lambda=Phi^T*D*(I-lambda*K)^(-1)*(r+K*a-a).

This yields a positive-definite symmetric part under the SAME fixed-policy, complete-history, fixed-linear-map and rank assumptions. At lambda1, A_1=Phi^T*D*Phi; the fixed point is ordinary occupancy-weighted terminal-return regression. This algebra does not turn the actual nonlinear selected-leaf learner into this baseline, and no new trajectory or parameter update is required to verify it.

## Mean-square qualification and an actual one-update bound

This is a distinct stability question, not another value-risk interval or training experiment. Write a complete fixed-process frozen-linear episode increment as c_n-B_n*theta. Its expected matrix is A_lambda above. Let theta* solve A_lambda*theta*=E[c], let e=theta-theta*, and xi=c-B*theta*. Under independent fixed-process episodes, E[xi]=0. If the symmetric part of A_lambda has minimum eigenvalue mu>0, beta bounds E[||B||_2^2], and sigma^2=E[||xi||^2], then direct expansion plus ||u+v||^2<=2||u||^2+2||v||^2 gives

E[||e_next||^2 | e] <= (1-2*eta*mu+2*eta^2*beta)*||e||^2 + 2*eta^2*sigma^2.

Thus 0<eta<mu/beta is sufficient for a bounded second moment with limiting upper bound eta*sigma^2/(mu-eta*beta). This is a conditional bound, not vanishing error or a guarantee for the actual eta=.05 nonlinear learner. Bounded complete histories/features make beta finite, but do not give a useful positive numeric mu without the actual distribution/rank/conditioning. No empirical step-size choice is made.

Why this extra distinction matters: in an abstract one-observation fixed process, let phi=0 with probability99/100 and phi=10 with probability1/100, terminal0, linear scalar value phi*theta. The complete-episode matrix B=phi^2 has mean1. At eta=1/20, E[theta_next|theta]=(19/20)*theta, yet E[theta_next^2|theta]=(23/20)*theta^2. The mean converges while the second moment grows. These are exact closed-form moments; no repeated update, search, game state or current-rule realizability is asserted. They show a logical gap between mean convergence and stable trajectories, not an observed failure of the real Model.

For a length-N COMPLETE path, let P be the one-step shift on observation indices and Phi collect its fixed linear features. The path matrix is B=Phi^T*T_lambda*Phi with T_lambda=(I-lambda*P)^(-1)*(I-P)=I-(1-lambda)*sum_(k=1..N-1)lambda^(k-1)*P^k. Hence ||T_lambda||_2<=2-lambda^(N-1)<=2 (N>=1). If every feature has norm<=F and N<=H, ||B||_2<=2*H*F^2 and beta<=4*H^2*F^4. At lambda1, T=I and the bound improves to H*F^2. The apparent long eligibility sum does not require a 1/(1-lambda) blow-up in this complete frozen-linear matrix; TD-error cancellation matters. This still does not compute mu or bypass the stated process assumptions.

## Finite nonlinear fixed points and what stationary parameters mean

A conditional LOCAL statement is available without reversible dynamics. Keep fixed K, root law and fixed smooth value map p(theta) over complete-history decision states. Let J(theta) be its Jacobian and R_lambda=(I-lambda*K)^(-1). The expected frozen-episode drift is h(theta)=J(theta)^T*D*R_lambda*(r-(I-K)*p(theta)). If a finite theta* represents the full Bellman solution EXACTLY, the residual vanishes and differentiation yields

Dh(theta*)=-J(theta*)^T*D*R_lambda*(I-K)*J(theta*).

## Final local-stability qualification: sufficient does not mean necessary

Exact Bellman representability above is SUFFICIENT, not necessary for a locally stable expected-drift point. Write W=D*R_lambda and e=r-(I-K)*p. At a smooth stationary point h(theta*)=0, the full derivative is C-S, where S=J^T*W*(I-K)*J and C=sum_i (W*e)_i*H_i, with H_i the value Hessian. If the symmetric part of S has minimum eigenvalue mu>0 and ||C||_2<mu, the derivative still has negative-definite symmetric part. This is a conditional local ODE/small-step deterministic mean-field criterion, not a convergence theorem for stochastic changing-policy updates. For fixed material leaves, ||H_i||_2<=4*||phi_free_i||^2/(3*sqrt(3)*scale^2), so an upper bound on sum_i abs((W*e)_i)*||H_i||_2 can qualify the curvature term. Neither the actual distribution, mu nor this inequality is established for historical runs; no new parameter clipping or search is implied.

A read-only actual-Model control makes the logical point concrete: two abstract one-observation histories have equal initial mass, identical free feature1, and opposite deterministic terminal payoffs+1/-1. K=0 and scale10. At theta0 the drift is0 and its derivative is-.01 although both Bellman residuals are nonzero (+1/-1); the finite difference agrees to1e-11. Their shared feature cannot represent both outcomes, but the averaged drift has a stable stationary point. This does not establish useful decisions, minimax labels, zero sampling variance or stochastic parameter convergence. `projected-stationary-control.json` records the arithmetic; no apply, Core, game or fitting was performed.

Conversely, with deterministic policies and a fully fixed random stream/internal state in a finite rule-terminated process, each state's actual policy return is a definite W/L/D. A finite nonterminal material tanh cannot exactly represent the +/-1 cases in real arithmetic; literal terminal-leaf constants are a different branch. Averaging over unfixed future opponent randomness can produce interior values, but mixing starting histories does not turn each fully conditioned state's deterministic return into that average. Neither this representability limit nor the counterexample is a reason to reject authorized empirical learning or invent a new approval gate.
