# Normalize once after integration: a task-law choice

The native interface implements w_m=E[Q_m(G)]/E[Q_Q(G)], not
E[Q_m(G)/Q_Q(G)]. This distinction follows from its declared common deadline
law; normalization is a single positive scale AFTER aggregating every task.
It never changes the raw aggregate material ordering for ongoing children.
Both interpretations remain possible alternative modeling assumptions; they
are not interchangeable implementations of the same law.

For G supported in(0,1), let q(G)=Q_Q(G)>0 and v_m(G)=Q_m(G)/q(G).
Then w_m=E[q v_m]/E[q] is expectation under the tilted law with density
q/E[q] relative to the original law. In particular
w_m-E[v_m]=Cov(q,v_m)/E[q]. Normalizing each component before mixing instead
equalizes the total Queen capability contribution of slow and fast deadlines.
The interface does not do that. A harmless final common scale can be moved
outside an expectation only when it is constant or the covariance vanishes;
no general commutation follows from all values lying in[0,1].

An exact illustrative control uses G=1/4 and3/4 with equal weights, chosen here
to expose the identity, NOT as a third fitted deployment law or a new candidate.
Use the already proved exact native R/Q histograms. Compute both interpretations
as rational numbers; because q and R/Q strictly increase between the two points,
the covariance is positive. A fixture may then place a rational decision
threshold between them. Such a reversal illustrates distinct objective weighting,
not a new legal use outcome, human price or reason to select whichever wins.

The exact R values for this control are aggregate58688/63027 (0.93115649)
and pointwise873637744/994283883 (0.87866027), a gap of about0.05249622.
This is materially larger than rounding error and follows solely from the
different task weights; it is not evidence that either objective is superior.

For a distribution with mass at0, q(0)=0 and per-sample normalization is undefined
there. The integrated constructor remains defined provided E[q]>0; the tilted
law simply gives zero mass to zero-capability cases. This is another reason not
to silently replace the aggregate formula with a pointwise ratio. The current
continuous linear law has no atom at0, but the boundary matters for transfer.

Interval bounds also require care: dividing by the exact integrated Queen is
sound here only because Q is known exactly and dominates all other native
contact statistics. With an uncertain maximum or mode-dependent law, dividing
individual lower/upper endpoints by a guessed maximum need not preserve a
shared material scale. Missing generic modes do not inherit this qualification.

Tests use exact fractions for the two-point identity and all predeclared native
laws, and demonstrate undefined pointwise normalization at0 without adding any
new game state, outcome observation, production prior or fitted parameter.
