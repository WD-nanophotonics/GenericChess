# Price identifiability in the current capture-only fragment

The current 4x4 experiment has one knight and two bishops (one on each side), in addition to the kings. Captures remove actors; no promotion, drop, actor creation or ownership transfer occurs. The compiled catalog also lists an unused rook. The result below concerns the actors actually present and their capture closure, rather than the catalog text.

Fix a target side. Every ongoing material leaf has score `720*(n*r+b)`, where `r=N/B>0`. Because the single knight can survive or be captured, its signed coefficient is either zero or its fixed ownership sign. The two bishops contribute a signed difference in `{-1,0,1}`. Captured subsets therefore give a finite overapproximation of all possible leaf coefficients, without looking at WDL labels or fitting prices.

For any pair of leaves, ordering can change only where `(n-m)*r=c-b`. Enumerating the actual 32 TRAIN inventories for both target perspectives gives positive crossings **1 and 2**. Thus the exact material ordering has five cells: `(0,1)`, `{1}`, `(1,2)`, `{2}`, and `(2,infinity)`. The boundaries are separate cells because equality introduces additional tied actions.

Within an open cell, all pairwise leaf comparisons and equalities are unchanged. Actual terminal WIN/LOSS values are positive/negative infinity and actual draws are zero, independently of price. Induction through every finite min/max tree preserves the optimal action set and its deterministic first action. Alpha-beta comparisons inherit this invariance under exact arithmetic and fixed node cutoffs. This is a statement about completed search and ordinal traversal, not a guarantee that separate wall-clock runs complete identical work.

The original frozen grid `[.25,.5,1,2,4]` contains two representatives of `(0,1)` and none in `(1,2)`. Therefore `.25` and `.5` cannot identify two different exact material policies in this fragment. The existing exact trees pass 504 within-cell action-set comparisons. A separately frozen, node-only software qualification checks the actual chooser after the current complete-game producer closes; its outcome is reported separately.

This does not recommend adding a price or repeating the old experiment. The five cells are an expressibility description, not a fitted value table. In particular, the old TRAIN means at `.25` and `.5` were equal, while baseline1 was lower, so this observation does not establish a missed profitable update. The strict-neighbor optimizer can in principle stop inside a flat cell before reaching a different cell, but no unobserved improvement is imputed here.

The same analysis explains why convergence to a unique numerical ratio is too strong a requirement for this finite evaluator. Policy equivalence, decision-cell membership and independently measured actual W-L are more informative. The previously selected `.25` and `2` lie in distinct cells; the lack of a DEV gain remains a negative result, rather than being explained away as simple rescaling.

This derivation does not cover drops, promotion, ownership changes, more actors, other piece types or a different evaluator. Those can introduce more coefficient vectors and new boundaries. Floating overflow/underflow and comparisons arbitrarily near a boundary also require separate qualification. A stronger rule-based proof service remains independent of material price, but its wall-clock completion can vary. None of these results makes UNKNOWN a draw or proves whole-policy W-L equivalence.
