# Estimands and primary-source limits for cheap material learning

The policy learner estimates a maximizer of expected capped proxy return over the declared roots, colors, opponent and mature search implementation. The return-regression model estimates a distribution-conditioned predictor from earlier count differences. These are different questions; neither automatically identifies an intrinsic causal piece price or the usual human ratio. A fixed distribution-averaged price vector remains a legitimate candidate even when individual positions with identical material have different returns.

Unit-count payoff is immediately indifferent to equal-number trades of different types. Through subsequent legal policy consequences it can still distinguish pieces, so this observation is not an impossibility proof. Actual Task174 measures that distinction rather than assuming it absent. Likewise, a reported mature-search mate is not a material-evaluation invocation count, and common-scale action changes alone cannot identify which fixed pruning margin caused them.

The final-quantity objective in Task175 is deliberately a proxy experiment. True game termination remains respected, but final quantity includes those terminal positions. The mature search still prioritizes actual mate. This evaluator/task-goal mismatch is retained and must be measured before treating a learned proxy improvement as winning strength or human-price recovery. No final quantity labels or reference prices modify the search's royal legality.

Primary sources checked on2026-10-12:

- Stockfish's official SPSA guide describes short time controls as faster approximations and emphasizes meaningful parameter movement relative to randomness; rare scoring features can be difficult to tune. It does not establish rapid recovery of human material ratios for our capped objective. [Official guide](https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Creating-my-first-test.html#spsa-tests)
- YaneuraOu's author describes an optimizer finding king-location rewards that an opponent at short time control failed to punish, and the difficulty of finding feedback against an opponent too strong to beat. This is a concrete motivation for checking adversary/task distribution, not evidence our Chess prototype succeeds or a reason to transfer Shogi semantics. [Author's article](https://yaneuraou.yaneu.com/2020/12/02/make-evaluate-function-10/)
- Texel's own Materialistic option explicitly uses common1/3/3/5/9 values. Using its output as the learning target would import those prices; it cannot serve as independent recovery evidence. We did not run or download it. [Engine README](https://github.com/peterosterlund2/texel#readme)

All source summaries are background, separate from our actual engine/game evidence. No human game data, NNUE teacher labels, extra workers or result-driven game extension were introduced.
