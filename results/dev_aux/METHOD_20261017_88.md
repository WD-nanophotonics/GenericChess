This is a retained-implementation comparison, NOT literal historical reproduction:
TDLeaf freezes one model per episode, sums lambda=.95 eligibility with eta=.05;
TreeStrap freezes each search, uses the STATIC features at every returned interior
node, and applies the MEAN half-squared one-sided transformed loss after that
search. All returned interior nodes count in the denominator, including zero
loss. Targets are constants; root/PV gradient is not substituted for another node.
Both use tanh(material/10), fixed pawn1 and four random coefficients. Tanh changes
loss geometry while preserving interval orientation. Same numeric eta does NOT
match step norms, update frequency, target type, total updates or training data.
Thus this estimates complete training-regime utility at equal searched-node cost.

The primary paper uses linear/summed all-node updates and alpha-beta one-sided
bounds; it also had many positional features, qsearch, search enhancements and
separately chosen step sizes. Our material-only/noq/noTT/mean variant cannot
establish or refute that paper's full result. Source: [Veness et al.2009](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2009/file/389bc7bb1e1c2a5e7e147703232a88f6-Paper.pdf), sections3-6.
