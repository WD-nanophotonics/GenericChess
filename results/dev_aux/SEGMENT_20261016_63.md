## Why additional piece types/counts alone are insufficient

### Exact leaf-order restriction (tasks1917/1918)

All32 original one-R/one-N setups and capture-only/no-creation/no-drop/no-promotion rules were checked. Reachable material leaves have dR,dN in{-1,0,1}; these nine vectors are a SAFE overapproximation, not all claimed reachable. Positive rho=R/N leaf-order crossings can occur only at1/2,1,2.

Within an open interval, a strictly increasing utility map preserves all leaf weak orders. It commutes with min/max on a fixed COMPLETE finite tree/horizon, so optimal action sets are identical, with policy identity under fixed price-independent tie-breaking. Actual terminal WIN/LOSS must outrank ALL finite material leaves and DRAW=0. The script's numeric terminal sentinels check4->2 only; they are not a bound for arbitrarily large rho.

rho2 is a boundary. Its leaf order is a nondecreasing collapse of rho4; on the same complete tree, best-actions-at4 are a subset of best-actions-at2. Tie resolution can change choices; this does NOT imply identical actual Native2/4 policies or fitness. Mature selective search/SEE/futility margins, variable trees/depths and deadlines violate the fixed-tree premise. Chance-node expectations also need a different argument.

Finite inventories refine finitely many intervals; they do not guarantee a unique continuous optimum. Conversely, finite open plateaus alone do NOT exclude an isolated optimum at a boundary: fixed tie-breaking can combine different tied choices across inputs. Task1924 supplies an explicitly ABSTRACT finite-horizon/price-free-continuation counterexample to that overstrong logical inference, not a legal GenericChess game or training/WDL evidence. A useful stable interval, not universal unique-price recovery, is the practical goal.
