## Why a high-price plateau is possible

With no promotion, actor generation or ownership transfer, let a root have n+ own Knights and n- opposing Knights, k=n++n-. At every descendant, signed Knight count lies in[-n-,n+]. For two nonterminal material leaves, their difference is delta_r*R+delta_n*N, where abs(delta_n)<=k and nonzero integer delta_r has magnitude at least1. For N>0 and R>kN, its sign is determined by delta_r, or by delta_n if delta_r=0. All pairwise rankings and ties remain fixed.

Draw0 is included as coefficient(0,0); mate/loss sentinels retain priority throughout the declared price domain. Hence a complete fixed finite min/max tree with fixed tie order cannot choose a different action above this bound. This algebraic conclusion is not inferred from a sample; enumeration checks implementation premises. All64 complete two-ply trees,5007 leaves, satisfied the conditions. R419 and1718 exceed the uniform270 bound. R262 does NOT exceed270 for k=3, so this proof alone does not explain the whole empirical262 plateau. No general claim covers selective mature search, promotions, drops or changed ownership. Sources1048–1051.
